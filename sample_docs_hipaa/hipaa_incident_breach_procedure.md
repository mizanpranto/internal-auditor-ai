# Riverbend Family Clinic - Incident, Breach and Contingency Procedure (FICTIONAL SAMPLE)

Version 1.5 - Owner: Security Official and Privacy Official

## 1. Reporting incidents

Anyone who suspects a security incident, such as a lost device, a phishing email or an unusual access, must report it to the Security Official immediately. Each incident is logged in the incident register with the date, systems affected and actions taken.

## 2. Response

The Security Official classifies each incident within one working day and leads the response. The response contains the incident, preserves evidence and restores normal operation. After a significant incident, a review is held within two weeks to record the cause and the corrective actions.

## 3. Breach risk assessment

When protected health information may have been used or disclosed in a way the Privacy Rule does not allow, the Privacy Official completes a documented breach risk assessment. The assessment considers the nature of the information, the person who received it, whether it was actually viewed or acquired, and how far the risk has been reduced. An incident is treated as a breach unless the assessment shows a low probability that the information was compromised.

## 4. Breach notification

Affected individuals are notified in writing without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery of the breach. For breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals, the Privacy Official reports them to the Secretary of HHS within 60 days of the end of the calendar year. The procedure does not yet describe notification of the media for larger breaches.

## 5. Business associate notification

Business associate agreements require the associate to notify the Clinic of a breach without unreasonable delay and in any case within 30 days of discovery.

## 6. Backups and recovery

The EHR is backed up every night and a full copy is kept off-site in encrypted form. Backups are restore-tested twice a year. A disaster recovery plan lists the critical systems and the order in which they are restored. The plan has not been exercised in the last 12 months.

## 7. Emergency operation

If the EHR is unavailable, staff follow the downtime procedure and use printed patient schedules and paper forms stored in the front office. A break-glass account allows a clinician to reach a record in an emergency, and every use of it is reviewed by the Security Official the next working day.
